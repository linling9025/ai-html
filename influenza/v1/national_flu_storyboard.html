01

Southern States Are the Epicenter of This Week's Surge

The national uptick isn't uniform — it's being primarily driven by explosive growth in Texas, Florida, Georgia, and Louisiana. These four states alone account for nearly 40% of the week's new cases.

What's happening? Holiday travel, indoor gatherings, and relatively warmer weather (which keeps people indoors with air conditioning) have created a perfect storm for transmission. If you're in the South or recently returned from there, be extra vigilant.

H3N2 Is the "Main Villain" — And It's Tough on Seniors

Nearly 7 out of 10 flu tests are coming back positive for Influenza A (H3N2). This matters because H3N2 seasons historically correlate with higher hospitalization rates, especially among adults over 65.

Good news: This year's vaccine includes good H3N2 coverage. If you haven't gotten your shot yet, it's not too late — protection kicks in within two weeks.

Young Children and Elderly Face the Highest Hospitalization Risk

While flu affects everyone, hospitalization data reveals a stark pattern: children under 5 and adults over 65 are being admitted at rates 3-4x higher than other age groups.

This week, the 65+ hospitalization rate hit 14.2 per 100,000 — the highest we've seen at this point in the season in 5 years. Families with young children or elderly members should prioritize precautions.

What Should You Do This Week?

💉
Get Vaccinated (If You Haven't) The flu shot takes ~2 weeks to provide full protection. With activity surging, now is the time.
😷
Consider Masking in Crowded Indoor Spaces Especially if you're in a high-activity state or have vulnerable family members at home.

Stay Home If You Feel Sick Symptoms like fever, body aches, and fatigue? Rest at home to protect others and recover faster.
🧴
Practice Good Hand Hygiene Wash hands frequently, especially after being in public places or before touching your face.

Check on Elderly Relatives Adults 65+ face the highest risk. Make sure they've been vaccinated and know early warning signs.
🏥
Know When to Seek Care Difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain, confusion, or symptoms that worsen after improving — seek medical attention immediately.
